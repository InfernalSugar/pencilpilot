## Start with an egg

Seen from the front, a head is roughly an egg shape: wider and rounder at the top where the skull is, narrower at the jaw and chin. Draw that egg lightly, then add a vertical centre line from the top of the head to the chin. Every feature will be placed along it or mirrored across it.

These are averages, not laws. Real faces vary, and those differences are exactly what makes a likeness. But the averages give you a solid starting grid to compare against.

## Eyes go halfway

The single most common mistake in drawing faces is putting the eyes too high. We think of the eyes as being near the top because the forehead seems like “nothing”, but measured from the top of the skull to the chin, the eyes sit almost exactly **halfway** down.

Measure it on any front-view photo with your pencil and you will see it. If your faces look like they have a tiny skull and a long chin, this is almost always why.

## The three bands

The face itself (from the hairline down to the chin) splits into three roughly equal bands:

- hairline to **brow**,
- brow to the **base of the nose**,
- base of the nose to the **chin**.

Inside the bottom band, the line where the lips meet sits about a third of the way down from the nose. The corners of the mouth usually line up roughly under the pupils.

## Width and ears

Across the eye line, the head is about **five eyes wide**: one eye, a gap one eye wide, the other eye, and roughly one eye-width from each outer corner to the edge of the head. That middle gap is a quick check. Most people draw it too narrow.

The ears sit on the sides of the head between the brow line and the base of the nose. Use those two guide lines you already drew, and the ears will land at the right height every time.
