## Why lines get hairy

Hairy lines come from searching: lots of short strokes laid over each other while you feel your way towards the edge. It is a natural habit, and a light search is fine early on. The problem is when the searching *becomes* the final line, leaving a fuzzy edge that is hard to read.

## Dot, dot, line

Before drawing an edge, put a tiny dot where it starts and another where it ends. For a long curve, add one more dot at the point where it bends most. Now the line has a clear job: get from here to there.

Ghost the movement between the dots a couple of times, then draw it in one stroke at an even speed. Aim for the end dot, not the pencil tip; your hand follows your eyes.

## When a line misses

It will happen, and that is fine. Do not scrub over it with small corrections. Instead, draw a fresh, confident line next to it where it should be, then erase or undo the first one. One wrong line plus one right line is still cleaner than ten nervous ones.

## Light, then final

A good working order: search lightly with loose, faint lines while you find the shape. Once you are sure, draw the final line once, a little darker, on top. The light searching fades into the background, and the confident line is the one people see.
