## Build in a pause

Most drawings go wrong in one place, not everywhere. The trouble is that you stop seeing a mistake once you have stared at it for a while. Short, regular pauses keep your eyes fresh.

Every minute or two, lift your pencil and look at the drawing and the reference side by side, as if someone else had drawn it. Stepping back, or flipping the drawing to see it mirrored, makes errors jump out.

## Compare one area at a time

Imagine the same grid over the reference and your drawing, say three by three. Now compare one cell at a time: where does the edge enter this cell, and where does it leave? Small regions are much easier to judge than the whole subject at once.

## Line things up

Hold your pencil perfectly vertical at arm’s length and look along it. Which points in the reference sit directly above each other? The tip of an ear above the front paw, the corner of a roof above a door? Do the same with the pencil held horizontal.

These alignments give you free checks. If two points line up in the reference, they should line up in your drawing too.

## Fix the biggest problem first

When you spot several things, fix the largest one first, usually the area that is furthest off. Small errors often sort themselves out once a big one is corrected, and fixing details on top of a wrong shape only means fixing them twice.
