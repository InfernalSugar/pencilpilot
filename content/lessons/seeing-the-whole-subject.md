## Take an inventory

Before drawing, name the big parts of the subject. For a sitting cat: head, ears, body, front legs, tail. For a mug: body, rim, handle. It sounds almost too simple, but naming the parts is what stops one of them quietly vanishing from the drawing.

## Go fast first

Give yourself a minute to put down every part as a simple, light shape. No details, no shading, no fixing. A rough drawing with everything in it can be improved. A beautiful head with no body cannot, at least not without redrawing.

If you have ever filled a page with one perfect eye and run out of time for the rest, this is the habit that fixes it.

## Work the whole page at once

After the first pass, move around the drawing instead of finishing one area. Improve the head a little, then the body, then the tail, then back. Each part stays at the same level of finish, so the drawing always looks complete, even if you stop early.

## Check the list

Before adding detail, look back at your inventory and tick each part off on your drawing. Then compare the overall outline with the reference: does every bump and corner of the reference have a matching bump and corner on your page? If something is missing, add it now while the lines are still light.
