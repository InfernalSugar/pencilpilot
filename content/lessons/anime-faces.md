## A ball and a jaw

The anime style simplifies the head into two parts: a large round **cranium** and a small, tapered **jaw**. Start with a circle. From both sides of the circle, a little below its middle, draw two lines that angle in and down to a fairly small chin, below the circle. The jaw can be soft and rounded for a young or gentle character, or more angular for an older or sharper one.

Add a vertical centre line from the top of the circle down to the chin. It keeps the face symmetrical now and tells you which way the head turns later.

## Set the eye line low

Compared with a realistic face, the anime cranium is big and the lower face is compressed, so the eyes sit **on or a little below** the halfway point of the whole head. Draw a horizontal eye line there, across the centre line.

If the eyes end up high on the head, the face instantly looks off and older. Placing them low leaves a big, open forehead and a short lower face, which is a large part of what reads as this style.

## Big eyes, evenly spaced

The eyes are large: each one roughly a fifth to a quarter of the whole head’s height, with the upper lid drawn as a thick, confident stroke and the lower lid lighter or left out. Sit the bottom of each eye on the eye line, or let the line run through the lower half of the iris.

Space them about **one eye-width apart**, mirrored across the centre line. Too close and the face looks pinched; too far and it looks blank. The iris is usually a tall oval that touches or tucks under the upper lid, with a clear highlight on the side the light comes from.

## Small nose, small mouth

The rest of the face is kept very simple. The nose is often just a small mark or a short line, roughly halfway between the eye line and the chin. The mouth is a short line or small shape a little below the nose, closer to the nose than to the chin.

The ears sit at about the height of the eyes and nose, on the sides of the head where the jaw meets the circle.

## Turning the head

When the head turns, the guides turn with it. Imagine the centre line and eye line wrapped around the ball: the centre line curves towards the side the head turns to, and the eye line curves up or down when the head tilts. Place the features on the curved guides, and make the far eye a little narrower than the near one.

Practise the front view until the placement feels automatic, then turn the guides a little at a time.
