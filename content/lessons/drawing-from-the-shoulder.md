## Three hinges

Your arm has three hinges for drawing: the wrist, the elbow and the shoulder. Each one is good at a different size of line.

The **wrist** is precise but has a short reach, so it suits small details. The **elbow** handles medium curves. The **shoulder** gives the longest, smoothest arcs, which is what you want for outlines and long edges.

## Lock the small joints

For a long line, keep your wrist and fingers fairly still and let the movement come from the elbow or shoulder. It feels odd at first, as if you have less control. In fact, a big joint moving smoothly gives a steadier line than a small joint making lots of tiny corrections.

Hold the pencil or stylus a little further back than you would for writing, and lightly. A tight grip makes the hand shake.

## Find your speed

Very slow lines wobble, because every tiny tremor shows. Very fast lines miss their target. Somewhere in the middle is a comfortable, even speed where the line comes out smooth and still lands where you meant it to.

Try to keep the speed the same from start to finish. Stopping and starting halfway is what creates bumps and kinks.

## Ghost it first

Before you draw a line, hover the pencil just above the page and trace the path in the air two or three times. This is called **ghosting**. It rehearses the movement, so when you finally touch down, your arm already knows the way.

If a direction feels awkward, turn the page or your body so you are pulling the line in a comfortable direction. There is no prize for drawing everything from one angle.
