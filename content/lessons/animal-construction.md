## Three circles and a line

Most four-legged animals share the same plan. There is a big mass at the front (the **ribcage** and shoulders), a smaller mass at the back (the **hips**), and a **head** on the end of the neck. Start with a circle for each, sized and spaced by comparing them with the reference.

Then run one flowing line through them all: from the nose, along the neck and back, out to the tip of the tail. That is the spine, and like the action line on a person, it carries the pose. A dog sniffing the ground has a spine that dips at the neck; a cat stretching has one that arcs.

## Wrap it in a bean

Join the circles with a smooth outline: over the top of the shoulders, along the back, round the hips, and back along the belly, which usually tucks up in front of the back legs. The result looks like a **bean** lying on its side. Squash it for a pig or a bulldog, stretch it for a greyhound or a weasel.

Add the neck as a thick tube from the ribcage up to the head, then block the muzzle onto the head circle as a small box or cylinder. Ears and tail come later.

## Mark the joints

Draw the legs as simple lines first, with a small circle at every place they bend. The key difference from a person: dogs, cats and horses stand on their **toes**. So the joint halfway down the back leg that points backwards is not a knee bending the wrong way. It is the **ankle**, called the **hock**. The real knee sits higher up, close to the belly, and points forwards.

The front leg works the same way in reverse: the elbow sits just behind the chest and points backwards, and the wrist sits low, a little above the paw.

## Check, then add the animal

Before any fur, check the big relationships. Is the ribcage really that much bigger than the hips? Is the head the right size compared with the ribcage? How long are the legs compared with the depth of the body? Answer these with the shapes, where fixing is easy.

Then draw the outline over the construction, and add the features that make it a particular animal: the shape of the ears, the length of the tail, the thickness of the neck. Those are what people recognise, and they only work when the body underneath is right.
