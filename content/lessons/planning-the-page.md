## Read the reference’s layout

Before you draw, look at the reference as a picture, not a subject. Where is the middle of the subject? How much empty space is above it, below it, and on each side?

You do not need exact numbers. “A bit left of centre, with more space at the top than the bottom” is plenty.

## Mark the middle

Put one tiny, light mark where the centre of the subject should land on your page. Everything else will be arranged around it, so it is worth a second look at the reference before you move on.

## Four marks: top, bottom, left, right

Now place four short ticks for the subject’s highest, lowest, leftmost and rightmost points. Check the space between your marks and the edges of the page against the space you saw in the reference.

These four marks decide the size and the position of the whole drawing in about ten seconds. If something looks cramped or lost in the page, move a mark now. It is the cheapest fix you will ever make.

## Stay inside, or decide to move

As you draw, the marks act as fences. When a shape wants to cross one, stop and look. Sometimes the mark was wrong and you should move it on purpose. More often the shape has grown, and pulling it back keeps the whole drawing in proportion.
