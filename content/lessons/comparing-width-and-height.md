## Find the four extremes

Every subject, however complicated, has a highest point, a lowest point, a leftmost point and a rightmost point. Find those four first. Ignore everything else for now.

Join them up with light straight lines and you get an **envelope**: a simple box or polygon that the subject fits inside. It is the first shape worth getting right, because everything else lives inside it.

## Compare with one pencil

Hold your pencil at arm’s length, horizontal, and measure the shorter side of the envelope with your thumb. Keeping your thumb in place, turn the pencil and see how many times that length fits into the longer side. One and a half? Just under two?

The exact number matters less than the comparison. You are asking one simple question: **is it tall, square or wide, and by how much?**

## Draw the box first

Lightly draw the envelope on your page with the same proportions. Measure it again before you move on: if the reference is one and a half times wider than tall, your box should be too.

It is common to squeeze wide subjects and stretch tall ones without noticing. Catching it at the box stage costs a few seconds. Catching it after the details are in costs the drawing.

## Build inside it

Now work inside the envelope. When a part seems to want to poke outside, check before you let it. Either the part is wrong, or the box is, and the reference will tell you which. Once the drawing is done, erase the box or let it fade under your final lines.
