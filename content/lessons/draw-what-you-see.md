## Notice the symbols

Your brain stores quick symbols for things: a cup is a rectangle with a handle, an eye is an almond with a circle. They are useful for recognising things, but when you draw from them, your lines stop matching the reference.

The fix is not talent. It is checking the reference more often than the symbol in your head.

## Look more than you draw

Try this rhythm: look at the reference, draw a short section of edge, look again. Your eyes should spend at least as much time on the reference as on your page. Long lines drawn from memory are where outlines start to drift.

For each section, ask three questions. Where does this edge start? Where does it end? Is it straight or curved, and which way does it bend?

## Follow the edge, not the idea

Trace an edge with your eyes slowly, as if you were an ant walking along it. Every time it changes direction, make a note of where. Those turning points are what make a shape recognisable, so get them in the right places and the curves between them become easy.

## Leave out what isn’t there

If you cannot find a line in the reference, do not draw it. Some edges are soft or disappear where a light shape meets a light background, and that is fine. Construction lines and guides are welcome while you work, but keep them light and erase them once the real edge is in place.
